Choose whole-house RO only after establishing why RO-treated water is needed beyond selected drinking outlets. A house-wide complaint does not automatically require this process. **Whole-house RO is a complete supply project**, often involving pretreatment, storage and pressure delivery as well as the membrane.

## Compare the required treated volume

For a kitchen, estimate drinking, cooking and any supported refrigerator demand. For whole-house treatment, identify all included branches and daily use, plus simultaneous peak demand. These are different design scales.

An illustrative kitchen using 3 product gallons daily needs 1,095 product gallons yearly. A hypothetical house using 250 product gallons daily needs 91,250. These are example inputs, not household averages. Applying the same assumed 50% overall efficiency gives 2,190 versus 182,500 feed gallons annually, before any water use excluded from that efficiency boundary.

That calculation explains why treatment scope belongs in the quote. It is not a prediction for a particular RO system. Use the [recovery guide](/guides/ro-system-recovery-rate-explained-for-homeowners-what-it-means-for-your-water-use/) to establish what the efficiency figure includes.

## Ask for a complete whole-house design

| Design question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Treatment target | Water analysis and supported reduction process |
| Membrane protection | Feed limits and appropriate pretreatment |
| Production | Output under actual feed conditions, not just a headline rating |
| Peak delivery | Storage and pressure arrangement, if needed |
| Product-water suitability | Assessment of distribution materials and any post-treatment |
| Reliability | Response to power loss, alarms, low storage or service |
| Waste and maintenance | Discharge arrangements and full component schedule |

Do not assume a high daily production number means the system directly supplies multiple showers at once. The supply design must connect production over time to the household's short peak draws.

## Under-sink examples cover a narrower job

The [Waterdrop G3P800](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0987FCQQW?tag=betterwaterguide-20) illustrates powered tankless under-sink RO. The [iSpring RCC7AK](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005LJ8EXU?tag=betterwaterguide-20) illustrates a conventional tank-based arrangement with remineralization. Neither is a whole-house substitute. Our [head-to-head comparison](/vs/waterdrop-g3p800-vs-ispring-rcc7ak-tankless-speed-or-simpler-ro/) examines their kitchen ownership tradeoffs.

If a non-RO device has the appropriate claim for the actual need, compare that option before accepting the extra water and service requirements of RO. A cramped kitchen cabinet is not by itself a reason to treat the entire house.

## Evidence boundary

[EPA's WaterSense RO specification](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-11/ws-products-watersense-ro-systems-specification.pdf) explicitly covers point-of-use systems and excludes point-of-entry RO. Do not transfer a kitchen-product label to a whole-house installation. This comparison supplies scope arithmetic and questions for a qualified designer, not a universal engineering design.
