A whole-house filter treats the water passing through its installation point before that water reaches downstream branches. “Whole-house” describes **coverage**, not a universal contaminant-removal claim. Untreated outdoor lines, bypasses and plumbing after the filter can change what reaches an individual outlet.

## Follow one gallon through the installation

Think of the system as a sequence: source water, isolation controls, selected treatment stages, distribution branches and fixtures. The order of stages must match the water and equipment requirements. A filter installed before the water heater can affect its incoming cold supply; a kitchen-only unit cannot do that.

Draw the branch points on paper. Mark outdoor taps, refrigerator, kitchen cold tap, water heater and any bypass. This simple coverage map prevents assuming that every outlet is treated merely because a tank sits near the main line.

## Identify the job of each component

| Component | Typical role | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Sediment element | Capture particles within its specified performance | Does not establish dissolved-contaminant or microbial safety |
| Carbon stage | Specific adsorption/reduction duties | Chlorine, chloramine and other claims require exact evidence |
| Ion-exchange softener | Reduce hardness through ion exchange | Not a general contaminant filter |
| UV treatment | Specified microbial treatment under stated conditions | Does not remove nitrate or hardness |
| Bypass assembly | Route water around equipment for service or other specified use | Bypassed water has not received that treatment |

Some packaged systems combine several duties. Count verified outcomes, not vessels or stages. A three-vessel system with unsuitable claims is not a better answer than one appropriate treatment stage.

## Flow and treatment are separate specifications

A housing's maximum permitted flow is not necessarily the flow at which its installed media achieves the intended reduction. Check the complete assembly's pressure loss and treatment rating at the household's simultaneous demand. Backwashing equipment also needs sufficient supply and drainage during its service cycle.

Use the [flow-rate worksheet](/guides/whole-house-water-filter-flow-rate-requirements-what-to-calculate-before-you-buy/) before choosing equipment. Nominal pipe or cartridge dimensions alone do not establish usable performance.

## What changes over time

Particle loading can increase restriction. Treatment capacity can be exhausted without an obvious pressure change. UV and other powered equipment have their own service and alarm requirements. Each duty therefore needs its own maintenance trigger, based on the installed manual and water conditions.

Before buying, establish exact replacement parts, removal clearance, shutoff access and who services the system. A bypass is not a license to use untreated water where treatment is needed for safety.
