Whole-house sizing is complete only when an offer meets the treatment need, demand, pressure budget and service constraints together. Use this page to **compare quotes on the same evidence**, after calculating demand. It is not another bathroom-count sizing chart.

## Put each offer into the same matrix

| Requirement | What a usable quote supplies |
|---|---|
| Treatment objective | Exact constituent or outcome and supporting model-specific claim |
| Service flow | Flow at which that treatment claim applies |
| Hydraulic performance | Complete-system loss at your agreed demand |
| Capacity | Rated duty, feed conditions, gallon/time limits and service trigger |
| Service cycle | Backwash/regeneration demand and drainage where relevant |
| Installation | Dimensions, clearance, controls and utility requirements |
| Ownership | Approved parts, replacement costs and responsible service provider |

Keep empty fields marked “not supplied.” A quote should not win because an unknown value was silently treated as unlimited capacity, zero loss or no maintenance.

## Work through a comparison

Suppose your agreed demand scenario is 8 GPM. Offer A advertises “12 GPM maximum” but gives no treatment-flow or pressure-loss evidence. Offer B documents the required claim at 8 GPM and supplies the relevant system curve. B is currently more assessable; A needs clarification before it can be compared. That is an evidence distinction, not proof that A is physically inferior.

Now suppose B requires a backwash flow the source cannot deliver. It also fails the proposed installation until that constraint is resolved. A favorable treatment number cannot cancel an unmet service requirement.

## Separate capacity units

Gallons of rated contaminant reduction, grains of softening capacity, media volume and dirt-holding capacity describe different things. Do not compare them as though a larger printed number means a larger useful system.

For carbon, use the [claim-specific capacity worksheet](/guides/whole-house-carbon-water-filter-capacity-planning-what-to-size-and-how-to-match-your-household/). For demand, use the [fixture-overlap worksheet](/guides/whole-house-water-filter-flow-rate-requirements-what-to-calculate-before-you-buy/). Keep actual source-water conditions beside every capacity estimate.

## Ask for an acceptance brief

Before ordering, agree what will be checked at commissioning: correct equipment and route, operating conditions, required flushing/sanitation, hydraulic delivery and treatment verification appropriate to the claim. Also agree what happens if the measured conditions differ from those assumed in the quote.

This creates a practical boundary between a seller's estimate and a supported installation. It avoids treating an upstairs shower complaint as proof of undersizing when the source, regulator, local plumbing or a loaded cartridge could be responsible.

[NSF's standards overview](https://www.nsf.org/consumer-resources/articles/standards-water-treatment-systems) supports matching exact treatment claims. The comparison matrix and worked case are editorial decision tools, not hands-on product test results or a substitute for a qualified system design.
