Buy a kitchen RO system by matching a documented water-treatment need to a supported installation. **Stage count and a large gallons-per-day number are not a buying specification.** First decide whether RO is necessary at all: a filter with the appropriate verified claim may meet a narrower need with less water use.

## Write a one-page purchase specification

| Requirement | What to write down |
|---|---|
| Treatment target | Measured contaminant or specific aesthetic problem |
| Evidence | Exact model's certified reduction claim and performance sheet |
| Supply | Pressure under use, temperature range and water analysis relevant to the manual |
| Daily use | Drinking, cooking and any connected appliance demand |
| Largest draw | Pitcher, cooking pot or several bottles filled together |
| Installation | Faucet opening, drain connection, cabinet space, service clearance and any required power |
| Ownership | Approved replacement codes, change rules, warranty and local service |

A general NSF/ANSI 58 reference does not mean that every possible contaminant claim is included. Use the [certification checklist](/guides/nsf-water-filter-certification-42-53-58-401-claims/) to match the particular claim and complete model.

## Separate daily production from the next glass

A rated daily production figure describes specified test conditions. It does not tell you how quickly an empty cooking pot fills in your kitchen. Request a faucet delivery figure and its conditions, plus refill behavior after a large draw.

With a tank system, usable stored volume differs from the tank's nominal shell size and changes with operating conditions. With tankless equipment, instantaneous production, startup behavior and any automatic flushing matter. Compare the actual draw you need, not a theoretical day of continuous membrane operation.

Measure the installed envelope with hoses, valves and cartridge-removal clearance. A tankless unit may remove the separate storage tank while still needing electrical access and substantial service space. Neither configuration automatically fits every small cabinet.

## Check pretreatment instead of adding it automatically

Compare your source water with the chosen system's permitted feed conditions. Hardness, disinfectant, sediment and other constituents can affect what pretreatment is required. RO can reduce dissolved mineral content at its outlet; that does not make a kitchen unit a whole-house scale solution. A softener upstream is a design decision based on the water and equipment, not a compulsory accessory for every household.

Private-well households need an appropriate laboratory assessment and treatment plan. Do not assume a consumer RO unit makes a microbiologically unsafe supply safe. Follow local health advice and the product's intended-use restrictions.

## Make a three-year ownership comparison

For each shortlisted model, add equipment, installation, approved replacement parts, expected water and sewer use, and any electricity or service charge. Keep quoted costs separate from assumptions. Mark unknown replacement prices explicitly; do not turn missing figures into zero.

Use the [RO recovery explanation](/guides/ro-system-recovery-rate-explained-for-homeowners-what-it-means-for-your-water-use/) before comparing efficiency claims. The [APEC ROES-50 review](/reviews/apec-roes-50-review/) shows the tradeoffs of one conventional tank system; it is not a universal recommendation.

## Source basis and limits

This is a specification worksheet, not a hands-on product ranking. Verify the current exact model, claims and installation manual before ordering.
