A sediment filter and a softener solve different measured problems. **Purchase priority is not the same as plumbing order.** You may need only one; when both are required, the system design commonly places suitable particle protection ahead of equipment that needs it. Follow the assembled equipment's requirements rather than a universal diagram.

## Build the evidence before choosing an order

| Evidence | What it supports | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness result with units | Size and need for a hardness-management decision | Whether particles or microbes are present |
| Visible or captured particles | Investigation of particle source and treatment | A complete water-quality assessment |
| Softener feed-water requirements | Whether pretreatment is required for that unit | The right cartridge without flow/load data |
| Pressure readings at expected demand | Available pressure budget for added stages | A filter's chemical reduction capacity |

A jar that settles can show some particles, but a clear jar is not proof that sediment treatment is unnecessary or the water is safe. There is no single 7-grains-per-gallon purchasing threshold applicable to every household. Consider the measured hardness, appliance needs and maintenance preference.

## Separate three installation cases

**Hardness without a particle problem:** evaluate the softener on its own requirements. Do not add a fine cartridge merely because a package includes it.

**Particles without a hardness objective:** investigate the source and select particle treatment if justified. A softener is not a substitute for a sediment filter.

**Both problems:** ask the designer to specify pretreatment, the softener and measurement points together. Include the combined pressure loss at household demand and any regeneration or backwash requirements. A nominal micron number alone does not identify the best cartridge: area, media, rated efficiency and loading matter too.

## The quotation checklist

Request the source-water analysis, chosen cartridge and housing codes, pressure-loss data, softener feed limits and a drawing showing bypasses and untreated branches. Include service clearance, drain provision where required and replacement responsibilities. This gives you a way to compare two quotes without counting stages.

If water changes suddenly after utility work, follow the utility's advice before treating it as a permanent equipment problem. For a well that changes after flooding, rain or repair, use the [well-test planning guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-well-water-test-panel-for-your-home/) and local health guidance; a new sediment cartridge does not resolve possible contamination.

## Sources and limits

[NSF](https://www.nsf.org/consumer-resources/articles/standards-water-treatment-systems) distinguishes softening from other treatment claims. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/hardness-water) explains hardness and its household effects. These sources do not prescribe one treatment train for every home. Use actual water results and manufacturer instructions for the final design.
