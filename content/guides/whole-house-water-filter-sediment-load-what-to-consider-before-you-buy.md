Sediment load describes more than how dirty a cartridge looks. To choose a filter, establish **where particles originate, how the load changes and what the downstream equipment requires**. A smaller micron number is not automatically the better purchase.

## Keep a short evidence record

Record when the issue appears, which outlets show it, recent utility or plumbing work and the time since the last cartridge change. Note the exact installed cartridge and repeatable inlet/outlet readings where available. Photographs can document appearance, but they do not identify a contaminant or its particle-size distribution.

| Pattern | Investigation before buying another stage |
|---|---|
| One outlet affected | Local fixture or branch source |
| Several outlets after supply work | Utility or installer guidance and event history |
| Repeated well sand | Well/pump assessment and source-water testing as appropriate |
| Rapid loss across a known cartridge | Load, cartridge specification and operating-flow comparison |
| Color or haze without settling | Analysis beyond a visual jar test |

Do not assume a glass that clears proves safe water. Nor does a TDS meter measure suspended sediment load.

## Ask what the micron claim means

Obtain the cartridge's stated efficiency at the rated particle size and the test basis. “Nominal” and “absolute” labels are not useful comparisons without their definitions. Media area, construction and dirt-holding behavior also affect service life and pressure loss.

Select the required capture performance from the treatment objective and downstream equipment limits. Do not automatically use a coarse screen because particles look like sand, or relax required filtration to recover shower pressure. A screen, depth cartridge, pleated element and backwashing medium each need evidence that they suit the actual duty.

## Calculate service burden from observed use

If a cartridge costs an illustrative $30 and needs replacement every four weeks, about 13 changes cost $390 annually before labor or disposal. If another documented arrangement costs more initially but changes less often, compare the full cost. Do not assume the new service interval without evidence from similar water or an agreed commissioning assessment.

Backwashing equipment needs adequate wash flow and drainage. Washable cartridges need explicit manufacturer permission and instructions; “pleated” does not automatically mean reusable.

## When the source matters more than size

Repeated particles from deteriorating pipework or a well problem may need source correction. Adding housings can hide the symptom while increasing maintenance. For changed well water, follow [EPA testing guidance](https://www.epa.gov/privatewells/protect-your-homes-water) and local advice.
