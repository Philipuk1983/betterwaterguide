Estimate whole-house filter demand from realistic simultaneous use, then compare it with **both treatment-rated flow and pressure-loss data**. A bathroom-count table is a rough marketing shortcut; it is not a substitute for the actual installation requirements.

## Make three demand scenarios

List fixtures on the treated branch and use their documented or measured flow rates. Keep US gallons, Imperial gallons and liters distinct. For a simple volume-and-time measurement, see the [bucket-test method](/guides/how-to-run-a-bucket-test-to-check-whole-house-filtration-performance/).

| Illustrative scenario | Included draws | Total |
|---|---|---:|
| Quiet use | One 1.5 GPM tap | 1.5 GPM |
| Normal busy period | Two 2 GPM showers plus a 1.5 GPM tap | 5.5 GPM |
| Planned overlap | The same draws plus a 3 GPM appliance fill | 8.5 GPM |

These are assumed inputs to demonstrate addition, not standard fixture ratings. Replace them with your own figures. An appliance fill can be intermittent; distinguish a brief overlap from sustained demand. Do not add outdoor irrigation if it is actually on an untreated branch.

## Compare the full treatment path

At each scenario, ask whether the complete system can maintain its relevant treatment claim and leave enough pressure at the downstream fixtures. A maximum mechanical flow may exceed the claim-rated service flow. Several stages in series also add their losses at the same flow.

Record inlet pressure during demand, the complete-system loss and the downstream pressure requirement. If a seller provides only “up to 15 GPM,” request the conditions and a pressure-loss curve. A missing curve is missing evidence, not permission to assume no loss.

## Include service demand

A backwashing unit can require a different flow from everyday household service. Confirm that the source can sustain that cycle and that the drain arrangement supports it. Increasing tank size can increase service requirements; it is not automatically a cure for weak supply.

An installer may need a formal fixture-unit or code-based design calculation for the project. This homeowner worksheet helps communicate real use; it does not replace that design or prescribe a universal 20% allowance.

## Agree a measurable acceptance condition

Give the installer the chosen normal and peak scenarios. Ask for documented treatment performance, acceptable downstream delivery and a service plan under those conditions. If the existing house supply already fails the scenario, resolve that limitation before blaming or buying a filter.

[Pentair's housing data](https://www.pentair.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Technical_Sheet_BigBlue_Housings.pdf) illustrate why flow is paired with pressure loss and exact model identity. The [pressure-rating guide](/guides/what-to-look-for-in-a-whole-house-water-filter-s-pressure-rating/) separates those hydraulic questions from the assembly's maximum operating pressure.
