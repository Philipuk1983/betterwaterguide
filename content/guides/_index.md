Choose a treatment system only after defining the water problem and the demand it must serve. Well owners should begin with the [well-water test panel guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-well-water-test-panel-for-your-home/); a screen or TDS reading cannot identify every contaminant. For a whole-house system, calculate [flow-rate requirements](/guides/whole-house-water-filter-flow-rate-requirements-what-to-calculate-before-you-buy/) before choosing housing, media, or cartridge size.

Pressure and maintenance are separate design checks. Use the [low-pressure diagnosis checklist](/guides/whole-house-water-filtration-low-water-pressure-diagnosis-checklist/) and [filter-bypass guide](/guides/whole-house-filter-bypass-basics-for-maintenance-planning-what-to-know-and-when-to-use-it/) before blaming the treatment media. For drinking-water reverse osmosis, the [RO buying guide](/guides/reverse-osmosis-system-buying-guide-for-homeowners-what-to-check-before-you-buy/) explains the system decision, while the [RO waste-water ratio guide](/guides/ro-system-waste-water-ratio-what-homeowners-should-know-for-kitchen-use/) explains how product-to-drain ratios should be read. These guides do not treat filtration, softening, conditioning, and disinfection as interchangeable.
