Maintain whole-house RO pretreatment against the **requirements of the installed membrane, pump and prefilter assembly**. Do not use a universal 10–15 PSI change rule or a generic chlorine strip as the entire maintenance plan.

## Create a stage-specific service card

| Stage or requirement | Record from the system documents | Measurement or action |
|---|---|---|
| Sediment protection | Approved cartridge and maximum permitted restriction | Inlet/outlet readings at a repeatable operating flow |
| Disinfectant protection | Membrane tolerance and specified pretreatment | The required test method, range and sampling point |
| Pump supply | Permitted inlet conditions and protection controls | Confirm normal feed conditions; investigate alarms |
| Scale or metal control | Source-water limits and pretreatment design | Water analysis and the specified maintenance routine |
| Restart | Isolation, flushing, sanitation and commissioning steps | Follow the assembled system's documented procedure |

For a combined filter bank, gauges at its ends show total bank loss. They do not identify which cartridge is responsible. Label the measurement points so a later service visit compares the same section.

## Separate restriction from chemical protection

A clean-looking cartridge can be restrictive, while freely flowing carbon may no longer meet its treatment duty. Pressure readings and a disinfectant test answer different questions. A TDS meter does not establish carbon's remaining disinfectant-reduction capacity.

Confirm whether the supply uses free chlorine, chloramine or another disinfectant with the supplier and system designer. The test method must resolve the membrane's specified requirement. A result below a coarse test strip's reporting range is not proof that a more demanding limit has been met.

[DuPont's FilmTec technical manual](https://www.dupont.com/content/dam/water/amer/us/en/water/public/documents/en/RO-NF-FilmTec-Manual-45-D01504-en.pdf) illustrates why pretreatment and commissioning are tied to membrane-feed specifications. It is not permission to apply FilmTec procedures or limits to an unidentified residential system.

## Make before-and-after readings comparable

Record the date, operating state, approximate feed flow, inlet and outlet pressures, exact cartridge parts and relevant test results. Compare with the documented clean-system record under similar conditions. A larger pressure difference at a much higher flow does not by itself prove that a cartridge has loaded.

For example, 48 PSI before a bank and 44 PSI after it gives a 4 PSI difference. After service, compare another 4 PSI reading only if the operating flow is comparable. The calculation is simple subtraction; whether it is acceptable comes from the equipment limits and available pressure margin.

## Treat service and restart as separate jobs

Have the qualified service provider follow the system's shutdown, pressure-relief, electrical-isolation and hygiene procedure. Use only approved replacement parts, cleaning products and lubricants. Do not invent a sanitizer concentration or open a pressurized housing.

Before returning the membrane to service, complete the specified cartridge flushing and verify that feed conditions meet the documented requirements. A replaced cartridge is not a completed service if flushing, leak checks or the required water-quality checks remain undone. Keep the bypass state and any untreated-water exposure in the service record.

## Investigate an unusually short service interval

Repeated loading can indicate changed source water, an unsuitable cartridge, inadequate pretreatment or another system fault. Do not automatically install a coarser cartridge or increase pump pressure: either can defeat the original protection design.
