For confirmed hard water, distinguish **removing hardness**, **controlling scale**, and **filtering other contaminants**. They are separate jobs. A sediment or ordinary carbon filter does not become a softener because it serves the whole house.

## Turn the complaint into a purchase brief

Write three lines before comparing equipment:

1. **Evidence:** hardness result, units, sample point and date.
2. **Desired result:** reduced measured hardness, reduced scale deposition, or another named problem.
3. **Constraints:** service flow, installation space, drain, power and maintenance you can provide.

For example, “white deposits, no water test, no drain available” is not a completed softener specification. The next step is a hardness measurement and installation assessment. It is not an automatic instruction to buy a salt-free product.

## Read hardness in the right units

[USGS classifies hardness](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/hardness-water) as soft at 0–60 mg/L as calcium carbonate, moderately hard at 61–120, hard at 121–180 and very hard above 180. These are descriptive categories, not mandatory purchase thresholds. USGS also cautions against using a national map to make a property-level decision.

Keep the original units on the report. If a controller asks for grains per gallon, follow its documented conversion and any iron or manganese adjustment. A TDS meter reading is not a hardness measurement, and spots on one glass do not establish the water chemistry.

## Match the result you actually want

| Desired result | Equipment category to investigate | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Lower calcium/magnesium hardness in supplied water | Ion-exchange softener | Usable capacity at its programmed salt setting, service flow and inlet limits |
| Scale-control benefit while hardness remains | A specifically documented conditioner | Exact process, applicable test evidence and water-chemistry limits |
| Capture suspended grit | Sediment treatment | Particle duty and pressure loss at the required flow |
| Reduce a named drinking-water contaminant | Treatment with that specific verified claim | Exact model, replacement component, rated capacity and operating conditions |

[NSF's standards guide](https://www.nsf.org/consumer-resources/articles/standards-water-treatment-systems) distinguishes softening under NSF/ANSI 44 from other treatment categories. A claim about materials or structural integrity alone does not establish scale reduction or removal of a particular contaminant.

## Compare installation feasibility before brands

Have the proposed location checked for the system's required drain arrangement, power, inlet pressure, temperature, bypass and service clearance. Do not assume a generic stage order fits every source or product combination. Equipment manuals and the water analysis should determine the design.

If the necessary arrangement cannot be installed, choose another design that still achieves the required result. Lower maintenance is valuable only when the substitute actually addresses the problem. A kitchen taste filter may be appropriate for a separate drinking-water concern, but it will not soften water supplied to a shower or washing machine.

Once measured demand and installation constraints support a salt-based system, the [low-maintenance softener shortlist](/best/best-whole-house-water-softener-for-low-upkeep-what-to-choose-and-maintain/) compares named options and ownership tasks.

## Define how you will judge the result

For softening, use an appropriate hardness test at the treated point under the system's instructions. For scale control, use the product's defined claim and verification method; unchanged hardness is not by itself evidence of conditioner failure. For health-related contaminants, obtain appropriate testing and professional interpretation.

This guide supplies a decision brief, not a treatment design for an untested home. No appliance-life extension, energy saving or medical benefit is assumed.
